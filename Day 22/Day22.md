# Day 22 – Validate Your Startup Idea Like a VC

## Challenge Overview

Today I used Claude to perform a professional startup validation exercise for my startup idea.

---

# Prompt Template:

Act as a Startup Advisor, VC Analyst, and Market Research Expert.

Help me validate my startup idea.

First ask me:

1. Startup Idea
2. Problem Being Solved
3. Target Customers
4. Why I Want to Build It
5. Existing Validation (users, feedback, waitlist, etc.)
6. Target Market/Country
7. Startup, Business, or Side Project?

After collecting my answers, generate a professional PDF-ready Startup Validation Report containing:

* Executive Summary
* Problem Validation
* Founder-Market Fit Score
* TAM, SAM, SOM Analysis
* Competitor Analysis
* Market Gap Analysis
* Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
* Buyer Persona
* Customer Pain Points
* Buying Triggers & Objections
* Customer Journey
* Risk Assessment
* Pivot Opportunities
* Go / No-Go Recommendation
* 30-Day Action Plan

Use tables, scores, charts, and professional consulting-style formatting.

Output should look like a report prepared by McKinsey, Y Combinator, or a VC firm and be ready for direct PDF export.

# Startup Idea

**Landr AI**

An AI-powered interview preparation and career growth platform that provides personalized mock interviews, resume reviews, technical assessments, and career guidance for students and early-career software engineers.

---

# Executive Summary

Landr AI aims to solve the problem of inefficient interview preparation among engineering students and job seekers.

The platform combines AI-powered interview simulations, resume analysis, skill-gap identification, and personalized learning recommendations.

The analysis indicates strong founder-market fit and a growing market opportunity due to increasing competition in technology hiring.

# Key Learnings

* Assumptions should be validated before development.
* Customer interviews provide better insights than opinions.
* Founder-market fit is critical.
* Market gaps often exist within crowded industries.
* A focused MVP is more valuable than a feature-rich product.
