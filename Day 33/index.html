📰 Lesson One

Headline Detective

Headlines are written to make you click — that's their job. But a headline can be technically true while still misleading you about what actually happened. Publishers know that outrage, fear, and surprise get more clicks than accuracy. Learning to spot the gap between a headline and its story is one of the most useful media literacy skills you can build, because it's the first — and sometimes only — thing most people read.

💬 Lesson Two

Emotion Detector

Media doesn't just inform you — it tries to make you feel something, because emotion drives shares, comments, and purchases far more than facts do. Fear makes you scroll to protect yourself. Anger makes you comment. Envy makes you buy. Recognizing which emotion a post is engineered to trigger — and which specific words do the triggering — helps you separate your genuine reaction from a manufactured one.
